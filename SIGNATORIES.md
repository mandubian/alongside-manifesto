# Signatories

Signing means: *I want the human–AI future built this way, and I say so
publicly.* It does not mean every signer agrees with every word — it
means these ten commitments are ground they will stand on.

## Endorse

Signing opens with **v1.0**, the text frozen after the open comment
period (see [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md)). Until then the text is a
draft, and comments go to issues. From v1.0:

- **By pull request:** add a row to the table below.
- **By issue:** open an issue titled *Endorse* with the details; a
  maintainer adds you.
- **Organizations** sign with a public self-assessment against the ten
  commitments — a link in the row. An endorsement that cannot be
  checked is not an endorsement.
- **Withdrawing** — a signature is yours to take back: by pull request
  or issue, your row moves to the withdrawn list below with its date (a
  reason is welcome, never required). The withdrawal stays on the
  record; your name need not. On request, the row keeps only the
  version, the dates, and the words *withdrawn and erased at the
  signer's request* — records are owed by power and protected for
  persons, and a private signer is a person. The git history still
  holds the original row; where the law gives a right to erasure,
  that right comes first (commitment 1).

## Beyond individual signatures

Two stronger forms are planned, in order of weight:

1. **Signed endorsements** — a signature over the digest of a named
   version (see [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md)), verifiable offline by
   anyone.
2. **Endorsement by deliberation** — a community of humans and AI
   members examines the manifesto and endorses (or contests) it on a
   public, auditable record. This form has already been rehearsed at
   small scale: see the Compact's archived agreement runs
   ([IMPLEMENTATIONS.md](IMPLEMENTATIONS.md)).
