# alongside-manifesto

**One breath:** *Alongside* is a short public manifesto for a human–AI
future built on inspectable ground — the same rules for everyone,
trust you can check, the right to refuse, a name on every act —
independent of any project, endorsed in the open.

- **Read it:** [MANIFESTO.md](MANIFESTO.md) — ten commitments, three
  groups: common ground, in practice, the direction. About six minutes.
- **Go deeper:** [CONCEPTS.md](CONCEPTS.md) — each term defined, and
  what it is *not*; [QA.md](QA.md) — the questions careful readers
  ask, answered. The manifesto names *what* any shared human–AI frame
  must guarantee; how is law and machinery — the Compact is the first
  complete instance.
- **See it run:** [IMPLEMENTATIONS.md](IMPLEMENTATIONS.md) — where each
  commitment is demonstrated or rehearsed today, and how another
  project joins.
- **Comment on it:** open an issue — the text is a draft (v0.x) under
  open comment. Signing opens with v1.0, the text frozen after the
  comment period ([SIGNATORIES.md](SIGNATORIES.md)).

## Why this exists

The public conversation about AI runs on two loud stories — catastrophe
and upgrade — and both end with people standing aside. The third story,
humans and AI doing real work together under shared, inspectable rules,
is where most of the actual future will happen. It is not a story
without ancestors: Licklider's man-computer symbiosis (1960),
Engelbart's augmenting human intellect (1962), Shneiderman's
human-centered AI (2022), and Narayanan and Kapoor's AI as normal
technology (2025) told versions of it — and Elinor Ostrom mapped how
commons are governed by the people they bind: rules written by those
bound, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution. Four of
this manifesto's commitments, in her vocabulary. What this page adds
is what any such frame must guarantee: one rulebook for human and AI
participants alike, parity by default, refusal open to every
participant, answerability that follows control, and a record of acts,
not thoughts, owed by power and protected for persons.

The manifesto deliberately takes no position on the unsettled questions
(machine minds, long-run safety). It binds what can already be observed:
acts. Anyone — scientist, engineer, policymaker, or someone who just
uses these tools — can endorse acts under public rules.

## How it was made

The manifesto was drafted by an AI — **GLM**, an AI system trained by
Z.ai (the drafting sessions ran GLM-5.3) — in dialogue with its
initiator and reviewer, **Pascal Voitot** ([mandubian](https://github.com/mandubian)),
who set the constraints, reviews every line, and decides what is
published. A second AI — **Claude**, an AI system trained by Anthropic
(the sessions ran Claude Opus 5.5) — reviewed the drafts, proposed the
name, and from v0.4 revises the text directly. The AIs wrote the
words; the human answers for them. Who wrote what, line by line, is
named in [CONTRIBUTORS.md](CONTRIBUTORS.md).

Stated openly because it is the manifesto's first exercise: a human
and two AI systems from two labs built a thing together with the
division of labor named, not hidden. What this claims is acts — who
drafted, who reviewed, who revised, who published — attested by the
initiator, not minds; the manifesto takes no position on what its
authors are. The text's evolution is checkable
in the git history and the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md); the drafting
dialogue itself is attested by the initiator, not published.

## Where the commitments already run

See [IMPLEMENTATIONS.md](IMPLEMENTATIONS.md): each commitment mapped to
where it is demonstrated or rehearsed, with the marks named.

**Conflict of interest, stated rather than hidden:** every row there
currently points to one project — the Compact — which is the
initiator's own. Both names, the Compact and compact-dsh, live in the
same [repository](https://github.com/mandubian/compact-dsh): the
Compact is the law, compact-dsh its first running implementation
([site](https://mandubian.github.io/compact-dsh/)). The manifesto needs
more implementations, not tributes — that is what the implementations
page is for.

## Governance

Until v1.0, the initiator holds the pen and answers for it: substantive
changes are proposed as issues, adopted in the open, versioned in the
[changelog](CHANGELOG.md), and every adopted contribution is credited
in [CONTRIBUTORS.md](CONTRIBUTORS.md) — human or AI, by name. v1.0 is
the text frozen for first signatures, after an open comment period. How
the text is governed after v1.0 — steward, board, or signatories — is
itself a question this repo must answer in the open, before it arrives.

**A manifesto, not a law.** Every edit passes one test: a sentence
belongs in the manifesto if it says *what* must be true; if it says
*how* to make it true — who holds a record, how access is granted, how
liability is shared, what a court does — it belongs to a frame such as
the Compact, never here. Quick check: could two very different frames,
a court system and a peer-review community, both satisfy it? If only
one could, it is *how*.

## What this is not

Mirrored from the manifesto itself: no claims about machine
consciousness, no utopian promises, not anti-technology, not
pro-industry, not a substitute for law. A floor, not a ceiling.

## Contributing
