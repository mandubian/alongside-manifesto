# Issues — the queue before there is a repository

**Published 2026-10-31.** Each entry below is now a GitHub issue with
the same number — entry 1 is
[#1](https://github.com/mandubian/alongside-manifesto/issues/1),
through entry 10,
[#10](https://github.com/mandubian/alongside-manifesto/issues/10).
Discussion, and any new question, goes to the
[issues](https://github.com/mandubian/alongside-manifesto/issues).
This file stays as the record of how the queue began; what follows is
as it was written before publication.

This file is the issue tracker while the repository is local. Each
entry below is written ready to become a GitHub issue on the day of
publication; a decision on one lands in the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md)
and its entry here closes. Nothing on this page is settled — that is
what makes it a queue.

Items already decided live in the changelog and are not re-opened
here. Of v0.4's open items, the one still undecided — item 3, the
missing QA answers — continues here as entries 5–7.

## 1. Frame against frame

The manifesto says there should be many frames, and says nothing
about what happens when they meet: a participant under two rulebooks,
an act that crosses a border, two verdicts that disagree. Interop is
ungoverned territory. *Proposal:* start by collecting real collisions
as they occur; design ahead of evidence has a poor record.

## 2. The unborn

Every "say" in the ten belongs to someone alive and present. The
people who will live longest under these frames cannot sign, comment,
or refuse; their interests enter only through today's honesty. No
document solves this. *Proposal:* one sentence in *What this
manifesto is not* — or the admission that silence here is a choice
that should at least be visible.

## 3. Letter and spirit

A deployer can satisfy every commitment in letter and game it in
spirit: consultations that change nothing, records that exist but
inform no one, self-assessments that flatter. Commitments 4 and 7
make dishonesty expensive, not impossible. *Proposal:* a CONCEPTS
reading — a frame obeyed in letter and hollowed in spirit fails the
ten's intent, and the test is whether the say, the record, and the
contest *bite*.

## 4. A say that cannot bite

A case of entry 3, kept separate because it has its own proposal.
Commitment 3 can be met by a consultation nobody could attend —
language, literacy, time, power. A say that cannot change the outcome
is theater with minutes kept. *Proposal:* a reading under *public
rules*: the say is real only if it can be shown to have changed
something, or refused with reasons.

## 5. Military AI

Does the frame reach weapons systems — refusal by a weapons platform,
records for a strike, repair after one? The honest first pass may be
that some acts sit outside any frame the ten can imagine, and saying
*where the ten stop* is itself a claim the text has not made.
*Proposal:* an answer, either way, in QA.

## 6. Open weights, chains of answerability

When anyone can download, fine-tune, and deploy a model, who answers
along the chain — developer, fine-tuner, host, deployer, user?
Commitment 6's *control, not proximity* is the principle; the chain
needs its readings. *Proposal:* a QA answer mapping the principle onto
the open-weight chain.

## 7. The manifesto and the EU AI Act

The Act is law; the manifesto is a stance. Where do the ten already
hold by law in the EU, where does the Act fall short of them, and
where does it go further? *Proposal:* a QA answer, written by someone
who has read the Act in full.

## 8. Measuring what people become

Commitment 8 is the least measured of the ten: no longitudinal
measure of agency exists in any implementation. *Proposal:* a method
note — what would count as evidence that people became more capable,
collected by whom, over what time.

## 9. Governance after v1.0

Until v1.0 the initiator holds the pen. After signatures open: stewards,
a board, the signatories themselves? The README promises this question
will be answered in the open before it arrives. This entry is that
promise, tracked.

## 10. Companionship

QA draws the line: the companion relationship is private life, the
company that deploys a companion is power, and the ten bind it. What
the relationship itself might need is unwritten: what refusal means
for a companion, what a relationship owes the record, where
engagement becomes dependence by design, and how commitment 8 would
measure it. *Proposal:* gather what companion products and their
regulators already do, and what has gone wrong, before writing a
word; the territory is larger than one QA answer.

## 11. The review points not adopted — the discussion promised

CONTRIBUTORS promises a linked discussion, once the repository is
public, for the review points that were judged and declined. The
repository is public; the discussion is due. The declined points and
their recorded reasons live in the changelog — v0.2's "servitude" and
"both sides" (kept, with reasons) and the two-stories caricature
(taken in part) among them. *Proposal:* open the issue, list the
points with their reasons, and invite the argument to reopen any of
them; closing it leaves the record as it stands. Opened as [issue #11](https://github.com/mandubian/alongside-manifesto/issues/11).
