# Changelog

The public history of this text, for readers and signatories. The git
history carries the same story for collaborators; this page tells it
to the people who signed a version and deserve to know what changed
since. Signatures bind to a version — endorsing v0.1 is endorsing
v0.1, not whatever the text later becomes.

## 0.5.1 — 2026-10-31 (the handle convention)

## 0.5 — 2026-10-31 (a manifesto, not a law)

The initiator's direction: this is a manifesto, not a law, and the
Compact must not be rewritten into it. The text was audited against
that line, and what failed it left the manifesto. Written by Claude;
the principles are the initiator's.

- **Subtitle** — *One written law for humans and AI* read as if the
  text were the law. Now: *A manifesto for humans and AI under one
  written law.*
- **What this manifesto is not** — "Not a substitute for law" becomes
  **Not a law**: the text names what any frame must guarantee, never
  how; the rules, the courts, the records, and the code belong to the
  frames that answer it.
- **The what/how test**, written into the README's governance section
  and into QA: a sentence belongs here if it says what must be true; if
  it says how to make it true, it belongs to a frame.
- **Moved out of the manifesto** (each failed the test):
  - commitment 2's access rule (*examination follows role and need*) —
    now one reading in CONCEPTS; each frame designs its own;
  - commitment 6's liability formula (*in proportion to their power*)
    — now one reading in CONCEPTS; the principle stays: *the name
    follows control, not proximity*;
  - commitment 6's conditions for future AI accountability — now in
    QA, as a reading, with the reason they cannot be a rule (a stake a
    deployer assigns is the escape itself). The guard stays in the
    text. *(Open item 1, adopted.)*
- **Acts, not thoughts** — commitment 2: *the record holds acts, not
  thoughts — the same for every participant.* The initiator's
  principle, learned when an earlier project recorded agents' reasoning
  but could not record humans', and the frame was not symmetric. Where
  the line falls is each frame's to draw. New CONCEPTS entry, new QA
  answer, and an IMPLEMENTATIONS row (compact-dsh, clause R-10).
- **Parity, said openly** *(open item 5, adopted)* — commitment 1:
  *parity is the default*; commitment 5 made symmetric: *any
  participant, human or AI, may decline an instruction that breaks the
  agreed rules — without penalty*; *What this manifesto is not*:
  *parity in the rules is a choice about procedure, not a verdict on
  what AI is.* CONCEPTS and QA follow.
- **Open item 4 closed** — who may examine the record is *how*: each
  frame's to design.
- **Terminology** — QA and README called the manifesto "a frame";
  frames are what answer it. Now "a standard".

Still open: item 2 (standing for the affected) and item 3 (QA answers
for open-weight models, military AI, the EU AI Act).

## 0.4.1 — 2026-10-31 (participant restored)

- **Commitment 5** — v0.4 narrowed *"a participant that cannot say no
  is being used, not included"* to *"a person"*. The initiator
  corrected it: the sentence is meant for every participant, human or
  AI. Restored, and made explicit so no reader narrows it again: *any
  participant, human or AI, that cannot say no is being used, not
  included.* Same in CONCEPTS.
- **The intent, stated by the initiator:** to set humans and AI at the
  same level, as far as possible.

**Open — added to the list in 0.4:**

5. **Parity, said openly.** Today the intent is carried by vocabulary
   (*participant*, *included*), while the v0.2 asymmetry in commitment
   5 (*a safeguard the rules require* for AI, *a right the rules
   protect* for people) pulls the other way. Proposed: commitment 1
   states *parity is the default; every difference is written down and
   justified*; commitment 5 becomes symmetric (*any participant, human
   or AI, may decline an instruction that breaks the agreed rules,
   without penalty*); and *What this manifesto is not* adds that
   parity in the rules is a choice about procedure, not a verdict on
   what AI is.

## 0.4 — 2026-10-31 (the companion files catch up)

Written by Claude (Anthropic) at the initiator's request, after its
review of v0.2–v0.3; GLM paused meanwhile. Most of this round is
consistency: v0.2 changed the manifesto, and the files around it had
not followed.

- **Authorship** — Claude added to the manifesto's authors (top line,
  making-of, README, SIGNATORIES, CONTRIBUTORS): GLM drafted; Claude
  reviewed, named, and from v0.4 revises; the initiator reviews every
  line and decides.
- **Contradictions removed:**
  - "refusal and answerability run both directions" (README, QA) —
    false since v0.2: refusal is asymmetric, and only people and
    institutions answer today.
  - "this manifesto declines to answer" the personhood question (QA) —
    commitment 6 does answer it: *not today, and only if earned*. QA
    now says so.
  - "Rules that bind only machines, or only people, are not shared
    ground" (commitment 1) — false since the roles clause and
    commitment 5's asymmetry. Now: *a rulebook that exempts machines,
    or exempts people, is not shared ground.*
  - "records anyone can check / examine" (README, QA, CONCEPTS) —
    contradicted commitment 2's privacy clause.
  - Signing: the README invited signatures while governance reserves
    them for v1.0. Signing now opens with v1.0; until then, comments.
  - The deliberation endorsement was "demonstrated" in SIGNATORIES and
    "rehearsed" in IMPLEMENTATIONS; now rehearsed in both.
- **Wording** — commitment 3 (*secret law is no law* back in its place;
  *public is not enough*); 5 (disclosure tied to refusal: *no one can
  refuse what they cannot see*); 6 (pronouns that had no referent; the
  guard stated on its own: *accountability is never handed to a
  machine so that a person can escape it*); 8 (*building and running*;
  *less choice*, matching the agency measure); 9 (*the more is checked
  first*).
- **CONCEPTS** brought in line with v0.2: the rights floor, a say in
  writing the rules, a new *Disclosure* entry, answerability follows
  control, the agency measure and upstream costs, checking before
  irreversible acts; the typo "less call" gone.
- **IMPLEMENTATIONS** — the v0.2 clauses listed as *not yet shown*.
- **The manifesto states its version** under its subtitle, so copies
  that travel without this file still say what they are.
- **Attribution** — the *moral crumple zone* is Madeleine Clare
  Elish's term (2019), not the reviewer's; CONTRIBUTORS corrected.
  README names Shneiderman, and Narayanan and Kapoor.
- **Links** — QA and SIGNATORIES point to IMPLEMENTATIONS.md, not the
  README section that only points there.
- **Reading time** — the README promised three minutes; the text is
  now about 1,450 words, so it says about six. Moving the door out of
  commitment 6 (open item 1 below) would win some of it back.

**Corrections to earlier entries** (added here, not overwritten
there):

- *0.2* calls the review's versioning and changelog points "stale".
  They were current when written — the review read commit `1ba528c`,
  before `b08bd3a` added this file — and already done when they were
  triaged: parallel, not stale. (The reviewer is a party to this
  correction; the git history is the evidence.)
- *0.3* says no manifesto or movement claims the name. True as
  written, but an AI company uses it: Alongside (alongside.care), a
  mental-health chatbot sold to US schools. Not a collision of kind,
  but a search for "Alongside AI" finds it first, so the name should
  travel with its subtitle.
- *0.3* says the manifesto "declines" the personhood question; see
  above.

**Open — proposed, awaiting the initiator's decision** (each becomes an
issue when the repository goes public):

1. **Move the door out of commitment 6.** Keep the guard in the text;
   move the conditions for future AI accountability to QA. The
   condition "holding something to lose" can be gamed: a stake a
   deployer assigns to an agent — a wallet, a bond — is the very escape
   the guard forbids. And it is the most contested sentence in a text
   meant to be signed by everyone; QA's own line applies: *the guard
   matters more than the forecast.*
2. **Standing for the affected.** v0.2 gave a say to *the people a
   rulebook binds* but dropped *and those it affects*, with no reason
   recorded. People on the receiving end of an AI decision are not
   bound by the rulebook, so as written they have neither a say nor a
   defined standing to contest (CONCEPTS, *Contestation*).
3. **QA answers still missing:** open-weight models (who answers along
   the chain from developer to fine-tuner to deployer), military AI,
   and how this text relates to the EU AI Act.
4. **Who may examine the record.** "Examination follows role and need,
   never curiosity" protects persons, but a deployer can also use it to
   refuse scrutiny of what it did. Proposed: *what power did is open;
   who it touched is protected.*

## 0.3 — 2026-10-31 (the naming, and the fixes that rode with it)

- **The manifesto is named: *Alongside*** — one written law for humans
  and AI. Two rounds of review preceded it: the original title collided
  with a real industry association (Positive AI, France, 2022), and the
  four replacement candidates proposed next were dismantled for
  leaking *status* the text refuses — member, co-owner, ally. The name
  is the text's own first paragraph: AI systems act in the world,
  *alongside* people — a position, not a status: not above
  (domination), not beneath (servitude), the two endings commitment 10
  rejects. Collision check: no manifesto or movement claims the name
  (verified 2026-10-31). French: *côte à côte*. Credit: proposed by
  Claude (Opus 5.5) after the dismantling; assessed and adopted here;
  decided by the initiator.
- **Commitment 1** — the floor/ceiling image removed (it collided with
  "a floor, not a ceiling" in *What this manifesto is not*, a
  different meaning): now plain — *no shared frame overrides the
  rights people already hold.*
- **Ostrom joins the ancestors** (README): commons governance maps to
  four commitments — rules by those bound, monitoring, graduated
  sanctions, conflict resolution.
- **Two neighbours named in QA** (verified to exist): the Human + AI
  Manifesto (working-together principles) and the Pro-Human AI
  Declaration — with the difference stated where it is sharpest: where
  they answer the personhood question *never*, this manifesto declines
  the question and sets an earned-capacity door instead.

## 0.2 — 2026-10-31 (the review round)

An external review by Claude (Opus 5.5, Anthropic), 2026-10-31, was
triaged point by point; what follows is what changed. Adopted wording
is credited in [CONTRIBUTORS.md](CONTRIBUTORS.md).

- **Commitment 1** — the rights floor (*no rulebook stands above the
  rights people already hold: a floor beneath, never a ceiling over*)
  and the roles clause (*roles may differ; every difference written
  down and justified; no one exempt*).
- **Commitment 2** — records are *owed by power and protected for
  persons*; examination follows role and need, never curiosity. The
  surveillance question now has a QA answer.
- **Commitment 3** — public is not yet fair: the people a rulebook
  binds have a say in how it is written, not only how it is applied.
- **Commitment 5** — refusal restated as explicit asymmetry (a
  safeguard the rules *require* for AI systems; a right the rules
  *protect* for people) and the disclosure clause: no one has to guess
  whether AI was involved.
- **Commitment 6** — the name follows control, not proximity: those
  who design, deploy, and profit answer in proportion to their power
  over the act (the "moral crumple zone" risk, closed).
- **Commitment 8** — the measure is agency (*handing a task over is a
  choice; losing the choice is the harm*), and the costs that begin
  before deployment: data labelers, creators whose work trained the
  model, energy and water.
- **Commitment 9** — where harm cannot be repaired, the checking moves
  before the act: the less reversible the harm, the earlier the
  question.
- **Preamble** — "no position on whether AI is conscious, good, or
  safe" restated so it no longer reads as a stance against safety work.
- **README** — the lineage correction (this story has ancestors:
  Licklider 1960, Engelbart 1962, human-centered AI,
  AI-as-normal-technology; the claim "nobody has given words to" was
  wrong and is gone), the conflict-of-interest statement,
  IMPLEMENTATIONS.md split out with demonstrated/rehearsed marks and
  join criteria, a governance section, and the checkable-making note.
- **QA** — the neighbors answer rewritten honestly (OECD, UNESCO 2021,
  Council of Europe 2024, EU AI Act, Montreal Declaration's open
  consultation); new questions: surveillance, home use, can an AI
  sign.
- **SIGNATORIES** — organizations sign with a public self-assessment.
- **CONTRIBUTORS.md** — born: the contributor log, human and AI by
  name, adopted wording credited.

Not adopted from the review, with reasons: "servitude"/"both sides" in
commitment 10 were kept — commitment 10 itself states the interests
question is open, and the making-of plus QA already carry the
asymmetry; the review's two-stories caricature point was taken only in
part for the same reason (the text targets the public conversation,
not safety researchers). The reviewer's stale points (versioning,
changelog) were already done before the review landed.

## 0.1 — 2026-10-31 (pre-publication draft)

The whole drafting day, one version. Substantive decisions, in order:

- **The manifesto** — ten commitments, three groups (common ground,
  in practice, the direction), a *what this is not* section, and the
  closing. Written plain on purpose: three minutes for a scientist, an
  engineer, a politician, or an everyday user.
- **The making stated** — the text names its author (GLM, an AI system
  by Z.ai), its initiator and reviewer (mandubian), at the top and
  in full: an act on the record, not a claim about minds. The
  manifesto's first exercise is its own origin.
- **Commitment 4 added — no one is infallible** — the ground under the
  other nine: machines, makers, governors, rules, and this document
  all err; the errors that matter most come from whoever holds the
  final word. Numbering became 4-3-3 (ten commitments).
- **CONCEPTS.md and QA.md** — the precise telling beside the plain
  one: each term defined with what it is *not*; the careful reader's
  questions answered, including where the manifesto stops (it names
  *what*; law and machinery name *how*).
- **Commitment 6 extended — the door with a guard** — today only
  people and institutions can bear accountability; if AI systems ever
  can, it is *earned by that capacity, never assigned as an escape*.

Draft state: no signatures yet, no public release yet. v1.0 will be
the text frozen for first signatures.
