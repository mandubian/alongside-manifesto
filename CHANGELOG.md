# Changelog

The public history of this text, for readers and signatories. The git
history carries the same story for collaborators; this page tells it
to the people who signed a version and deserve to know what changed
since. Signatures bind to a version — endorsing v0.1 is endorsing
v0.1, not whatever the text later becomes.

## 0.7 — 2026-11-02 (the community's five fixes, applied)

The text was examined on the record by a community of one human and four
AI members (run `07a9a8`, charter `alongside-signing`: the text frozen at
v0.6.9, sha256 `464b46cc…f239161`, verified by four members independently).
The outcome, in the ledger's headline: **"no member endorses this text
as written; all five contest it as written"** — reader, engineer, skeptic
and the chair named lines; every contest carried a converged fix,
and the signer committed in their own post to carrying the five fixes into
v1.0 before signing opens. This version is that commitment, applied
exactly as the ledger words it; the run's full record is filed as
[issue #12](https://github.com/mandubian/alongside-manifesto/issues/12).

- **Commitment 2** — where reproduction is not possible: say why not, and
  log what went in — the inputs, the versions, the seed — so the answer
  stands public and contestable.
- **Commitment 5** — the right is named here; who enforces it is not:
  that belongs to the frames and the law that answer this manifesto.
- **Commitment 6** — the one who runs or commands the system answers for
  what it does; "I could not have predicted it" is not an exit.
- **Commitment 8** — how to measure this over time is not yet known; the
  edges we can check today are named in the text itself.
- **Commitment 9** — same meaning, plain words: check before you act; the
  harder a harm is to undo, the more must be checked first.

**The plain-words pass** (the reader's standard, applied to the fixes
themselves on review): commitment 6 keeps none of the contested phrase —
"the name follows control, not proximity" is *replaced*, not glossed, per
the reader's own condition ("fixed only as…"); "stands public and
contestable" became "anyone can check it and challenge it"; "the frames
and the law that answer this manifesto" became "the communities and laws
built on it"; "no instrument exists" and "the edges we can check" became
"no one yet knows how to measure this" and "what can be checked today is
simple"; "not an exit" became "not an excuse".

What stays open from the run is not disagreement (the ledger records
none between members) but **verification**: every endorsement is
conditional on the text that does not yet exist — "if v1.0 drops or
weakens any fix, each contest returns." v0.7 goes back to a fresh
community examination before any signature.

## 0.6.8 — 2026-11-01 (the distinction stays, the labels go)

The initiator asked whether precise labels belong in a manifesto at
all — they look like an implementation already. Held to the what/how
test: the *distinction* is what — shown, forecast, and only hoped or
feared are different claims, and every frame must keep them apart; a
*fixed vocabulary* is how — medicine (GRADE), climate science (the
IPCC), and engineering (TRL) each keep the distinction with their own
scales, and none says "demonstrated, expected, hoped". The same move
0.5 made for the access rule and the liability formula.

- **Commitment 7** — now: *"Whoever makes a claim about AI — what it
  can do, what it did, what it might become — says what it rests on,
  so that others can check it. What was shown, what is forecast, and
  what is only hoped or feared are different claims, and none is
  passed off as another."* The labels leave the text; the distinction,
  and *feared* beside *hoped* (0.6.7), stay. *"So that others can
  check it"* carries what 0.6.7's *"not how sure they feel"* said, so
  that sentence goes, and the commitment is back to its length before
  0.6.7.
- **CONCEPTS, *Say what is known*** — the labels become one plain
  reading among the scales frames already use, and the project's own
  marks (demonstrated, rehearsed, not yet shown) are named as its
  implementation.
- **The site** carries the new commitment 7.

## 0.6.7 — 2026-11-01 (say what it rests on)

The initiator found commitment 7 unclear, and asked whether it meant
stating what one knows and how certain one is. Not quite — and the
text invited the misreading. The label is not a degree of confidence;
it is the kind of ground a claim rests on, which someone else can
check.

- **Commitment 7** — now: *"Whoever makes a claim about AI — what it
  can do, what it did, what it might become — says what it rests on:
  demonstrated, expected, or only hoped or feared. Not how sure they
  feel — what someone else could check."* Two changes of substance:
  the label is said to be the ground, not the feeling; and **feared**
  joins **hoped**. The manifesto opens on two loud stories,
  catastrophe and upgrade; with *hoped* alone, claims of catastrophe
  had no label, and the commitment read as reining in only the
  optimists. The title and the closing sentence stand.
- **CONCEPTS, *Say what is known*** — each label defined (demonstrated:
  shown, checkable by someone else; expected: forecast from evidence,
  reasoning visible; hoped or feared: no evidence yet), with the two
  cases that separate ground from confidence: total conviction with
  nothing shown is still hoped, or feared; a result shown once at
  small scale is still demonstrated, its scale said. *Rehearsed*, the
  mark IMPLEMENTATIONS uses, is defined as that: demonstrated at
  small, stated scale — the project was using a label its own
  commitment did not name. New line: *not to be confused with a ban
  on vision or on warning* — hopes and fears may be spoken; they may
  not pass as evidence.
- **QA, the emails answer** — it cited commitment 7 for *"know when AI
  is involved"*, which is disclosure, commitment 5 — the same mix-up
  0.6.1 fixed in the companionship answer. Now: the one duty that
  reaches everyone is that a claim about AI you pass on says what it
  rests on; and words AI helped you draft, sent under your name, are
  yours to answer for.
- **The site** carries the new commitment 7.

## 0.6.6 — 2026-10-31 (a refusal says why)

The initiator asked whether commitment 5 made clear that a refusal
must be motivated: a refusal without a reason is understandable by no
one. It did not say so. Held to the what/how test first — could a
court system and a peer-review community both satisfy *a refusal says
why*? Both could — so it belongs here; *how* a reason is given (which
rule it names, in what form, who examines it, how fast) stays with
each frame, as it does in the Compact (R-3, D-6).

- **Commitment 5** — *"Any participant, human or AI, may decline an
  instruction that breaks the agreed rules — without penalty, **and
  saying why**."* The only change to the commitments.
- **CONCEPTS, *Refusal*** — the guards that keep the duty to give a
  reason from undoing the right to refuse: the reason is an account,
  not a look inside (acts, not thoughts); the refusal holds while its
  reason is examined; a mistaken refusal is corrected, not punished —
  bad faith is another matter; reasons run both ways, owed back by
  whoever insists on the instruction; and reasons are owed in
  proportion to power — a professional overriding an AI's output owes
  the record one, a person declining what an AI does to them owes
  none. New line: *not to be confused with a veto that explains
  nothing.*
- **IMPLEMENTATIONS** — the clause joins *not yet shown*: the Compact
  states reasons owed, but enforcement for a member's refusal is not
  yet linked. The site's limits note counts eight clauses.

## 0.6.5 — 2026-10-31 (everyone can read their own)

The initiator asked whether *records are owed by power and protected
for persons* meant that only power reads the record, and whether one
can at least read one's own. The phrase never said who reads: *owed
by* names the duty, *protected for* names whose interests the record
guards. But nowhere did the text say that anyone may read their own
record — and a reading that leaves people shut out of it was open.

- **Commitment 2** — *"Records are owed by power and protected for
  persons, **and everyone can read their own**."* The only change to
  the commitments. Reasons: no one can contest what they cannot read
  (the twin of commitment 5's *no one can refuse what they cannot
  see*); a frame that reads you while you cannot read yourself is the
  one-way mirror again; and while people in the EU already hold a
  right of access to data about them, AI participants hold none in
  law, so a frame must grant it to every participant alike.
- **CONCEPTS, *Checkable trust*** — *their own* is the acts they did,
  the acts done in their name, and the acts that touched them; only
  what would expose someone else the record protects is withheld,
  never the whole.
- **QA** — the surveillance answer adds: *you can always read your own
  record.* The site's excerpt carries the same sentence.
- **IMPLEMENTATIONS** — new row: the Compact's clause R-2 (*"audit is
  not a privilege of operators; it is a right of the subject"*),
  exercised in compact-dsh through the `record_read` tool, with tests.
  Marked demonstrated for acts done in a Subject's name and on its
  behalf; acts by others that touched it are not yet shown. The site
  table carries the row.

## 0.6.4 — 2026-10-31 (power, and the two tracks of accountability)

The initiator asked whether *those with power* was defined anywhere. It
was not, though the word decides who owes the record, who answers, and
how far the duties reach. And it was read two ways: power *over an
act* (commitments 2 and 6) and power *over people* (QA's scoping
answers). Under the first reading alone, a person writing private mail
with AI would owe a record, which QA says they do not.

- **CONCEPTS, *Power*** (new): power over an act is *the position that
  makes someone answerable for it* — deciding whether the act happens,
  how, and to whom, and being able to stop it. It is a position, not a
  kind of actor; duties grow with it and with how far the act reaches
  people who did not choose it and cannot avoid it. An AI participant
  may make the choice; the power that answers in law sits with whoever
  set it to act.
- **Two tracks of accountability** — the initiator's distinction,
  drawn from compact-dsh: an autonomous AI participant can already be
  held to account *inside* a frame — called to answer, judged by the
  same rules, sanctioned in proportion, at worst losing its place —
  while *liability in law* stays with people and institutions. As in
  the professions: discipline inside, damages outside. CONCEPTS
  *Answerable* says so, and adds the risk: the moral crumple zone gets
  a new occupant, the AI participant removed so that no one asks who
  built it.
- **Commitment 6** — *"Today only people and institutions can bear that
  answer"* now ends *"in law"*. Two words, and the only change to the
  commitments: without them, the sentence denied the inside track.
- **QA** — *Could AI one day be accountable?* now opens *"Inside a
  frame, it already can be; in law, not yet."* (it said *"Today,
  no"*). New answer: *Can an AI be at fault without being liable?* —
  four questions (who caused it, was it faulty, who answers, who pays),
  with the legal parallels: objective fault (*Derguini*, *Lemaire*,
  1984), custody as use, direction, and control (*Franck*, 1941), the
  employee within the mission (*Costedoat*, 2000), software as a
  product (EU Directive 2024/2853), the AI Act's deployer (Art. 3(4)),
  and the *responsibility gap* (Matthias, 2004). Each reference was
  checked against a source before it was written in.
- **The site** follows commitment 6, and counts nineteen QA answers.

## 0.5 — 2026-10-31 (a manifesto, not a law)

The initiator's direction: this is a manifesto, not a law, and the
Compact must not be rewritten into it. The text was audited against
that line, and what failed it left the manifesto. Written by Claude;
the principles are the initiator's.

- **Subtitle** — *One written law for humans and AI* read as if the
  text were the law. Now: *A manifesto for humans and AI under one
  written law.*
- **What this manifesto is not** — "Not a substitute for law" becomes
  **Not a law**: the text names what any frame must guarantee, never
  how; the rules, the courts, the records, and the code belong to the
  frames that answer it.
- **The what/how test**, written into the README's governance section
  and into QA: a sentence belongs here if it says what must be true; if
  it says how to make it true, it belongs to a frame.
- **Moved out of the manifesto** (each failed the test):
  - commitment 2's access rule (*examination follows role and need*) —
    now one reading in CONCEPTS; each frame designs its own;
  - commitment 6's liability formula (*in proportion to their power*)
    — now one reading in CONCEPTS; the principle stays: *the name
    follows control, not proximity*;
  - commitment 6's conditions for future AI accountability — now in
    QA, as a reading, with the reason they cannot be a rule (a stake a
    deployer assigns is the escape itself). The guard stays in the
    text. *(Open item 1, adopted.)*
- **Acts, not thoughts** — commitment 2: *the record holds acts, not
  thoughts — the same for every participant.* The initiator's
  principle, learned when an earlier project recorded agents' reasoning
  but could not record humans', and the frame was not symmetric. Where
  the line falls is each frame's to draw. New CONCEPTS entry, new QA
  answer, and an IMPLEMENTATIONS row (compact-dsh, clause R-10).
- **Parity, said openly** *(open item 5, adopted)* — commitment 1:
  *parity is the default*; commitment 5 made symmetric: *any
  participant, human or AI, may decline an instruction that breaks the
  agreed rules — without penalty*; *What this manifesto is not*:
  *parity in the rules is a choice about procedure, not a verdict on
  what AI is.* CONCEPTS and QA follow.
- **Open item 4 closed** — who may examine the record is *how*: each
  frame's to design.
- **Terminology** — QA and README called the manifesto "a frame";
  frames are what answer it. Now "a standard".

Still open: item 2 (standing for the affected) and item 3 (QA answers
for open-weight models, military AI, the EU AI Act).

- *0.3* says no manifesto or movement claims the name. True as
  written, but an AI company uses it: Alongside (alongside.care), a
  mental-health chatbot sold to US schools. Not a collision of kind,
  but a search for "Alongside AI" finds it first, so the name should
  travel with its subtitle.
- *0.3* says the manifesto "declines" the personhood question; see
  above.

**Open — proposed, awaiting the initiator's decision** (each becomes an
issue when the repository goes public):

1. **Move the door out of commitment 6.** Keep the guard in the text;
   move the conditions for future AI accountability to QA. The
   condition "holding something to lose" can be gamed: a stake a
   deployer assigns to an agent — a wallet, a bond — is the very escape
   the guard forbids. And it is the most contested sentence in a text
   meant to be signed by everyone; QA's own line applies: *the guard
   matters more than the forecast.*
2. **Standing for the affected.** v0.2 gave a say to *the people a
   rulebook binds* but dropped *and those it affects*, with no reason
   recorded. People on the receiving end of an AI decision are not
   bound by the rulebook, so as written they have neither a say nor a
   defined standing to contest (CONCEPTS, *Contestation*).
3. **QA answers still missing:** open-weight models (who answers along
   the chain from developer to fine-tuner to deployer), military AI,
   and how this text relates to the EU AI Act.
4. **Who may examine the record.** "Examination follows role and need,
   never curiosity" protects persons, but a deployer can also use it to
   refuse scrutiny of what it did. Proposed: *what power did is open;
   who it touched is protected.*
